This journey begins after an organization administrator has given you access
and at least one Asset has been released. It uses the product as an authorized
consumer; it does not require repository access, local bootstrap, or deployment
commands.

You will discover an authorized release, verify its provenance, use the action
defined for its Asset type, and finish with evidence and feedback. The numbered
sections below are the journey; no separate setup path is required.

## 1. Open the Assets catalog

Open **Assets** from the product navigation. **Available to me** is the default
scope and lists only released, non-withdrawn Assets that your current identity
may use. Search by task, role, or outcome, filter by Asset type, and choose the
exact item you need. Cards show the human owner, sharing state, current exact
version, and updated time; Skills also expose your personal Enabled choice.

**Created by me** is the owner's workspace and can include private working
copies that have not been released. Return to **Available to me** when your goal
is to consume an Asset rather than author one.

An Asset that does not appear may be unavailable, withdrawn, or outside your
current permission scope. The catalog does not reveal denied Asset names or
metadata.

## 2. Verify the exact release

Open the Asset and confirm:

- the type matches the task you intend to perform;
- the selected version is the release you expect;
- its availability permits new use rather than being withdrawn; a
  `Deprecated` badge can remain on compatible historical releases;
- the summary, audience, prerequisites, and expected outcome fit your work;
- **Version and provenance** identifies the release coordinate, date, and
  digest.

The release is immutable. Choosing a version does not switch to a mutable draft,
and a newer release never rewrites an existing Capability Pack pin.

State-changing actions and external model calls require explicit confirmation.
The Assistant cannot approve, publish, withdraw, change permissions, or execute
arbitrary tools on your behalf.

During a grounded answer, the Assistant may discover a Skill you are currently
allowed to use, load the instructions from its exact release, and read a bounded
supporting text file when needed. These reads do not install or execute the
Skill, and package metadata cannot grant the Assistant additional tools or
permissions. If no authorized Knowledge evidence is available, the Assistant
does not start this Skill flow.

## 4. Complete a Capability Pack

A Capability Pack joins exact Knowledge versions, Prompt templates, Work
instructions, or Skills into one ordered journey. Starting it creates a private
assignment for your actor; it does not modify the Pack release.

Open each reusable Asset component from the journey and mark an item complete
only after performing its required work. Knowledge components remain identified
by their pinned version. If a component is denied, the Pack reports an access
gap without exposing the hidden title, type, or count; ask the Pack owner to
review your access.

## 5. Verify evidence and send feedback

When a Prompt run or Assistant answer uses organizational Knowledge, inspect
its citations before relying on the result. Opening a citation asks the API to
recheck your current permission and the original evidence integrity. A result
you could access earlier may correctly become unavailable after a revocation.

Use the Asset's feedback control to report whether the release was useful,
incorrect, or incomplete. Feedback is stored against the exact release and sent
to its accountable owner; it does not mutate the release you used.

<VerificationBlock title="You have completed the governed journey when">

- you used an exact released Asset rather than a draft or mutable alias;
- every Knowledge result and Pack component stayed within your current access;
- any available citation pointed back to authenticated source evidence;
- your progress or feedback was recorded against the correct actor and release.

</VerificationBlock>
